## I. Introduction
Artificial intelligence has become a rather prominent topic in recent years with the many great advancements that it has had. What was once just a pipe dream bound to the realm of science fiction has become something so close to becoming reality as we see articial intelligence become much smarter and capable of producing rather possible products. It has, however, become quite controversial in some sectors, such as in creative spaces where it is often used to copy artstyles and mass produce art, bringing concerns for how creative jobs will evolve in the future. For our purposes, however, we will be focusing on one of the other controversial sectors for artificial intelligence, education. While artificial intelligence can be a rather useful tool as a repository of information, it is also commonly abused by students to do their work for them, making the point of education rather moot in many cases. It has become a boogieman that threatens the credibility of each student's work as not a product of their own efforts and learning, making it hard for teachers to assess a student's capabilities. For my field of study, computer science, it is often used to produce code for assignments. While I personally have not made use of AI in my work, it certainly provides a tempting shortcut for assignments, so that people do not have to memorize certain aspects of langauges and quickly produce a seemingly functional program. Software engineering could possibly take much less time as artificial intelligence is able to implement frameworks and general code for the coder. The problem comes from the total reliance on artificial intelligence, as it is still in a rather primitive state that can be prone to errors that humans might have been able to catch. Computers, after all, are still far off from the capabilities of the human brain in information processing. 

## II. Personal Experience with AI
I do not have any experience with using AI in my software engineering course because I felt that I should try to do things for myself in an effort to help learn the material. I also do not fully trust AI due to all the different news that pops up from time to time about its unreliability. I would worry that if I used AI, I would then have to spend a lot of time debugging and checking over its work so that it functioned properly, so it never really became an option for me. An important part of my software engineering course was a concept called "WODs" which stood for workout of the day. Despite the name, in practice, they tested coding capabilities with an added element of a time limit. At times it was homework, a practice exam, or an actual exam. I never ended up using AI for any of them due to all the reasons stated above. The added time element to the WODs also further distanced me from attempting to use AI because I worried I would waste tons of time trying to debug and fix whatever code it gave me.

Another aspect of the class was the occasional essay, like this one. I have never used AI for essay writing. Ever since the advent of modern artificial intelligence, every English teacher I had hammered home the idea to never use AI and would heavily penalize it, and they would know because they knew our writing. Of course, if you only ever used AI to write the second point would be moot, or I suppose if you trained a modern AI to write in your style, but I never really wanted to risk it. Each and every person has their own unique writing style and quirks no matter how miniscule it may seem, so using AI for that feels like robbing writing of that aspect. This class also had the vague requirement of interesting essays, so that felt like it would have been another annoying aspect to handle. 

For the final project of my software engineering class, I did have some assistance from Github's Copilot AI due to it being used in the checks for pull requests and such. It was pretty helpful in describing issues when a check failed, so I would take its advice in order to fix any errors it detected so that my code would not fail any of the tests. It was a very useful time saver to help isolate where exactly the issues were and what exactly was wrong with it so that I could just ship it out in time. At times it was a little hard to see exactly the issue was even with Copilot, due to the interconnected nature of code, but it was a very useful hint.

I didn't use AI for any other aspect of the class. When learning concepts, I have a general distrust of artificial intelligence to give me accurate information and I did not feel like prodding around different AI programs to help simplify its explanations, so I kinda went with trial and error in combination with different online forums or tutorials. I did not use AI for any form of questions because it did not seem particually useful unless I was actually completely and totally lost. If I have a question, I will just ask it without using AI. The only way I could see AI being useful is narrowing the topic down, which I would only need if I was completely lost. I technically did use AI for coding examples due to Google search using AI for its search results now, so when I would look up how to do stuff, I would just use the example that Google AI generated, like during the react WOD, I would search for the class names for buttons, and then follow the example that Google search results AI gave. It would save time and I would not have to go searching through different sites. Sometimes, the Google AI summary was too vague or too wordy, so I would have to go to other resources, but I got my fair share of use out of it. In my general distrust for AI I never used it to examine, write, or document code. When I had issues reading code, I would just go to other online resources, and sometimes just the Google AI summary if it was clear enough. Writing code seemed like something I would just have to practice myself to help keep the information myself. With documenting code, I just have an issue in general where I do not do that enough because I never really have had to, and I do not have much experience with group coding where we needed extensive documentation between us. Even if I did document regularly, AI would just be lazy shortucut, and I would probably just do it myself since I wrote the code so I know what it does. The only time I used AI for quality assurance was for the final project with ESLint errors that Copilot would explain to me so I could fix it and merge my branches. It was a nice timesaver there and helped narrow down where and what the issue was without me having to comb through the code extensively.

## IV. Practical Applications
If AI is allowed to be used, I can see it being pretty useful. With AI we could trim off a few of the more tedious aspects of coding, so creating just a basic framework or outline that coders can directly work on making functional. Implementing really easy features so that people can focus their brainpower on more difficult functionality. AI could also work as a discussion partner possibly, like a version of the coding duck that can respond, where you jsut talk through whatever issues you are having with AI and get ideas on how you can move forward and resolve them. Of course, there there is the issue of AI driving you down the wrong path, but we just have to hope that we catch ourselves before it goes too far. Other tedious aspects it could take over is quality assurance, so that it can check for bugs and minor syntax errors. No matter what level you are at, it is always going to be a lot to go voer your code to check what is possibly wrong, so if AI can do that it would be a major timesaver. For bigger issues, it might not be quite there yet, but it can at least serve as a conversation partner. AI has a lot of potential to be useful as long as we don't abuse it.

## V. Challenges and Opportunities
I have not used AI much in this course, so I have not really encountered issues directly caused by AI, but with the current state of AI, we know that it can be unreliable at times. Artificial intelligence is still in its budding stages and is prone to mistakes and hallucinations. Admittedly, this is all based on hearsay since I am not well versed in using AI, but it never hurts to show some caution when using it. The artificial intelligence is unable to handle information and context the same way human beings can. While we are able to keep track of multiple staese of information and logical cause and effect, the same cannot be said for artificial intelligence, so for the best results, we still need a human to help keep track of and review what is output. Artificial intelligence has the opportunity to make things so much easier for us. In that sense, it has also been weighing on my mind a bit whether I should take advantage of it or not.

## VI. Comparative Analysis
Traditional teaching methods and AI-enhanced approaches each have their own merits. Theoretically, traditional methods should force the student to be more knowledgable and proficient with whatever they are learning, which in the context of software engineering is their languages and syntax. After all you cannot build a working program without knowing how the underlying code works. In that sense, the addition of artificial intelligence becomes a crutch that makes retaining that information less important, thus lowering the actual learning going on as they have a machine that is capable of doing what they need for them. Sure, it may lead to a working product still, but whether the student actually understands what was created comes into question. Of course, this is all under the assumption that the students soley relys on the AI to do their work for them, rather than actively engaging with it for educational purposes. That isn't to say that traditional teaching methods don't have their own flaws though. Under traditional teaching methods, the process of learning is heavily dependent on the relationship between student and teacher as well as the student's natural learning ability. Unfortunately, not all students will be able to learn at the same pace, so teachers have to fill in that gap as much as they can, but then it becomes difficult to maintain the balancing act to help all students equally. Some students will fall behind in work, which logically requires that a teacher helps them and invests more time on them, but sometimes that help cannot be provided unless that student comes forward on their own. In the mean time, more talented students may be forging ahead, but that doesn't mean they don't have their own issues, which may not be able to reach the instructor if they are busy handling other students who need more help. With AI, as long as students use it as supplemental material for learning, it can be very useful. Artificial intelligence could possibly act as another source of information for students to engage with for their questions or issues and help set up a starting point for students to build off of. There is the danger of overreliance on AI if the students fails to meaningfully engage with what is given. In the specific setup of my software engineering class, we operated under a flipped classroom, where we were expected to learn on our own. In that scenario, AI can be pretty useful for each student to help engage with the material, so that they aren't limited by just whatever they are able to understand on their own until they next see the teacher. The software engineering class I took, also encouraged use of AI on assignments as long as we were forthcoming with how we used it. They also adjusted instructions to be harder to interpret with AI to varying results. In that scenario, it feels as if there is little to impede students from solely relying on AI and not learning the material, other than the natural unreliability of current artificial intelligence programs. We also have to consider how AI will become an important part of the future that we will have to live and work with eventually, so under that lens, perhpas it is useful to incorporate AI into the educational experience to practice and develop good habits.

## VII. Future Considerations
With the advancements of AI going further and further, it is without question that all aspects of life will need to account for AI, including education. AI will only become more accessible and intelligent so that students are more able to effectively use it to cheat, unless heavy regulation is put on AI. It is not inconceivable that it becomes more and more standard for AI to be used to develop code, whether for simple bug checking and quality insurance, simple and common code, or even developing code itself. Software enginerring education is certainly going to have to adapt to implementing AI, while still making sure studnets have a base level of knowledge about the field. As time goes on, certain information starts to become obsolete and niche as it becomes less and less useful due to the conveniences of our modern life. Whether software engineering will become something like that, or is able to be adapted is something we will have to see as time goes on. Logically, software engineering knowledge would still be useful as code is the underlying framework that allows for all these technologies to work, but we shall see if humanity still deems it necessary or allows itself to become lazy.
